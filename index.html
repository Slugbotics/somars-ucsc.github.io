About Us

Building the Full Autonomous Stack

SOMARS, short for Slugs for the Oversight and Management of Aerial Robotic Systems, is the competitive drone team at UC Santa Cruz and part of Slugbotics. We design, build, and fly UAVs that can run a full mission on their own, and we compete against engineering programs from across the country.

We come from computer science, electrical engineering, mechanical engineering, and plenty of other majors. Everyone gets to work across the whole stack, from carbon fiber airframes to the PX4 and ROS 2 software and the computer vision that guides the drone.

Competitions

Where We Compete

We enter two student UAV competitions, and we are building one aircraft to fly both. Each asks for a drone that can run a real mission with nobody at the controls.

01

C-UASC

Collegiate Unmanned Aircraft Systems Competition

We flew Caligula I here in 2026, and we are going back: C-UASC 2027 is in Encino, California, 4 to 6 June 2027. It offers six optional missions: waypoints, a timed circuit, a package drop, a package delivery, target localization, and package recovery. We are preparing one aircraft to fly all of them.

02

SUAS

Student Unmanned Aerial Systems Competition · AUVSI

We flew Caligula II at SUAS 2026 at Skyway Range in Tulsa, Oklahoma, with mission flights on 15 to 17 September 2026. The operator sends one BEGIN command, then the aircraft flies waypoint laps on its own, surveys a search area while streaming imagery to the ground, finds ground targets like a mannequin, a tent, and a car, lowers payloads by winch, and returns home, all inside 45 minutes.

Our Approach

Simulation first. Then the real thing.

Every algorithm flies in Gazebo before it touches the real drone. We crash less, and we get to try more ideas.

Autonomous Navigation

PX4 and ROS 2 fly the mission. We send one command and the aircraft handles the rest.

Computer Vision

A YOLO11n detector finds ground targets in real time, running on the Jetson with TensorRT.

Simulation Pipeline

PX4 SITL and Gazebo, plus an automated mission gate that every change has to pass.

Our Own Flight Stack

ROS 2 nodes and ground control software we wrote ourselves.

Previous Airframe

Caligula I

The airframe we built for C-UASC 2026. A bare carbon fiber quadcopter with its electronics out in the open. It got us flying, and it taught us what we needed next.

Current Airframe

Caligula II

The airframe we built for SUAS 2026, sized for that mission: a survey camera, a winch payload, and a yellow shell over the center body. Carbon fiber arms and landing gear underneath.

2026 to 2027 Season

Building From the Ground Up

Caligula I took us to C-UASC 2026. Caligula II took us to SUAS 2026 in Tulsa. Now we are building toward C-UASC 2027 with one aircraft that can fly both missions. The whole platform, airframe to software, is ours.
