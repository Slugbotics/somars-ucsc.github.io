Perception.

We teach the drone to see. Real-time object detection and navigation, from the air, with nobody at the controls.

About

Giving the drone
its eyes.

We are the Perception subteam. We write the computer vision that lets the drone see the ground below it, pick out competition targets, and tell the autonomy stack where those targets are.

Day to day, that means working with the onboard cameras, training detection models, and getting inference to run in real time on the drone itself. It has to keep working at altitude during a fully autonomous flight, not just on a laptop at the bench.

Focus Areas

What we work on.

Target Detection & Classification

We train YOLO11n to find and classify competition targets (shapes, letters, colors) in aerial imagery, then run it under TensorRT on the onboard Jetson.

Dataset Collection & Training

We label real aerial imagery in Roboflow, and when we need more, we render synthetic images from Blender assets and environments to fill the gaps.

Geolocation

Once we spot a target, we work out its GPS coordinates. We combine the camera geometry, the drone's pose (GPS plus attitude), and a ground-plane projection to get there.

Model Training Pipeline

We augment those datasets, train YOLO11n so it still detects and classifies targets accurately under real flight conditions, and export the result to TensorRT for deployment on the drone.

Technology

Our stack.

Framework Python and PyTorch for training. TensorRT for optimized inference on the NVIDIA Jetson.
Detection Model YOLO11n
Camera Arducam AR0234, 2.3 MP global shutter, 1920x1200 at 30 fps
ROS 2 Integration Control parameters pass between the Jetson and the Pixhawk over uXRCE-DDS (Micro XRCE-DDS)
Ground Truth Validation Roboflow datasets, plus simulated assets and environments we build in Blender
