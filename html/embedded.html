Embedded Systems.

Hardware, firmware, and autonomous control. We build the brain and nervous system of the drone: a Pixhawk 6X running PX4, a Jetson running ROS 2, and everything wired between them.

About

Where hardware
meets software.

We are the subteam that lives between hardware and software. We design and wire up the electronics that make the drone work: a Pixhawk 6X flight controller running PX4, an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano Super companion computer, two DroneCAN buses for the ESCs and sensors, the radios, and the power system.

We also write the ROS 2 autonomy that lets the drone navigate, hold steady, run the payload winch, and fly competition missions with no human pilot in the loop. If you like soldering as much as you like writing code, you will fit right in.

Focus Areas

What we work on.

Flight Controller Integration

We fly a Pixhawk 6X running PX4 (the v1.16 line) with our own patch series on top. We configure and tune it so it handles low-level stabilization, motor mixing, and attitude control, and our firmware build includes ELRS/CRSF RC support.

Sensors, Power & Communication

We run two DroneCAN buses: one for the four MAD AMPX 60A HV ESCs (throttle over PWM, telemetry over DroneCAN) and one for the sensors, which are RTK GNSS with a compass, a Holybro flow module with an AFBR-S50 rangefinder and optical flow, a CubeID Remote ID, and a power module. Video and data reach the ground over a Skydroid YL20 and GR01 link (2.4/5.8 GHz) working as a network bridge, backed by a 915 MHz telemetry radio, with a BotBlox Ethernet switch onboard.

Companion Computer

We run high-level autonomy on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano Super with JetPack 6.2.2 and ROS 2 Humble. It talks to PX4 over uXRCE-DDS using px4_msgs and takes in what the perception code detects. Our somars_gcs_bridge node carries MAVLink v2 to the ground station.

Autonomous Controls

We write the control loops, state machines, and mission planners that fly the drone through waypoints and drive the two-bay winch (a passive spool with a brake and pawl) from PX4 AUX outputs. The safety rules are fixed: 15 seconds of lost ground link triggers return-to-home, 90 seconds triggers flight termination, and nothing else ever ends a flight. We have also rewritten the whole stack in Rust, cutting memory from about 183 MB to about 70 MB; it is installed on the aircraft but disabled for now.

Software Stack

How it's built.

Our autonomy software runs on ROS 2 Humble and talks to PX4 over uXRCE-DDS. Here is how the layers fit together.

Flight Controller Pixhawk 6X running PX4 (v1.16 line) with our own patch series and ELRS/CRSF RC support
PX4 Bridge uXRCE-DDS link between the Jetson and PX4 using px4_msgs
ROS 2 Packages somars_vision, somars_controls, somars_interfaces, and somars_gcs_bridge
Sensor Buses Two DroneCAN buses: ESC telemetry on one, GPS, flow, rangefinder, Remote ID, and power module on the other
Guidance & Safety Waypoint sequencing, winch payload drops, return-to-home after 15 seconds of lost link, termination after 90
Perception Interface Listens for target detections from the Perception subteam
Ground Link MAVLink v2 through somars_gcs_bridge over the Skydroid network bridge and a 915 MHz telemetry radio
Ground Control Native Rust operator app, a terminal GCS written in Go, a web operator page, and QGroundControl
Rust Rewrite Whole stack (control, guidance, winch, terminator, ground bridge) in about 70 MB versus about 183 MB for Python; installed but disabled
